\title{When Can Offline Data Certify a Policy Improvement?\\
Contrastive Coverage for Sequential Decision Making}
\author{Anonymous submission}
\affiliations{}

\begin{document}

\begin{abstract}
Offline deployment often asks whether a candidate improves on a baseline, not for two absolute policy values. Standard off-policy evaluation nevertheless imposes coverage on each policy separately, which can be unnecessarily strong: shared unsupported behavior may cancel from the difference even when neither value is identifiable. We introduce \emph{uniform contrastive coverage}, a condition on the signed difference of candidate and baseline path likelihoods. In finite-horizon sequential decision problems, we prove that this condition is necessary and sufficient for uniform nonparametric identification of $V^\pi-V^{\pi_0}$, is strictly weaker than individual coverage, and reduces to ordinary coverage when the baseline is the logger. When it fails, we derive sharp partial-identification bounds on deterministic history trees whose width is exactly the discounted unmatched off-support mass. We then develop a direct contrastive Bellman recursion and contrastive augmented PDIS (CA-PDIS). CA-PDIS is conditionally unbiased for any independently fitted continuation model; the oracle model yields the efficient influence function and an exact decomposition of signed-PDIS variance into efficient uncertainty plus removable action-composition noise. Off-support signatures further partition a policy library into maximal pairwise-identifiable classes, and a simultaneous empirical-Bernstein rule safely selects after searching many candidates. CPU-only experiments verify the sharpness and variance identities, show an $8.49\times$ MSE reduction from learned augmentation, and demonstrate that multiplicity-aware CA-PDIS retains high certification power where uncorrected post-selection inference fails. Coverage is therefore a property of the comparison being asked, rather than of either policy in isolation.
\end{abstract}
