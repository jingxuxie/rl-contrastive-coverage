\title{When Can Offline Data Certify a Policy Improvement?\\
Contrastive Coverage for Sequential Decision Making}
\author{Anonymous submission}
\affiliations{}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Off-policy evaluation usually assumes that the logging policy covers every trajectory generated by each target policy. This can be unnecessarily strong when the estimand is a policy \emph{difference}: unsupported behavior shared by a candidate and a baseline may cancel even though neither policy value is identifiable. We formalize this observation through \emph{uniform contrastive coverage}, a condition on the difference between the two policies' path likelihoods. In finite-horizon sequential decision processes, we prove that this condition is necessary and sufficient for uniform nonparametric identification of the policy-value difference over a rich model class. It is strictly weaker than covering either policy separately, yet reduces to ordinary coverage when the comparison baseline is the logger. We derive a signed per-decision importance-sampling representation and a finite-sample lower-confidence certificate for safe deployment. When contrastive coverage fails, we give sharp partial-identification bounds on deterministic history trees; their width is exactly the discounted unmatched off-support path mass. Small bandit and sequential experiments validate the predicted identification transition to numerical precision. In random bandits, separately bounding two policy values produces intervals that are 7.5 times wider at the median and 38.1 times wider at the 90th percentile than the sharp contrastive interval. These results show that coverage is a property of the estimand, not necessarily of each policy in isolation.
\end{abstract}
