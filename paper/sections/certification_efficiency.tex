\section{Finite-Sample Certification}

Under \UCC, define the signed per-decision contribution
\begin{equation}
Y_{\rm PDIS}=\sum_{t=1}^H\gamma^{t-1}
W_t^\Delta(Z_t)R_t.
\end{equation}
Unlike subtracting two independently bounded estimates, the subtraction occurs inside every episode and preserves both covariance and support cancellation.

More generally, let $Y$ be any episode contribution with $\bbE_\beta Y=\DeltaV$, deterministic range at most $B$, and sample variance $S_n^2$. Define
\begin{equation}
r_n(\delta)=
\sqrt{\frac{2S_n^2\log(4/\delta)}{n}}
+\frac{7B\log(4/\delta)}{3(n-1)}.
\label{eq:bernstein}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Deployment certificate]
\label{prop:certificate}
With probability at least $1-\delta$,
$|n^{-1}\sum_iY_i-\DeltaV|\le r_n(\delta)$.
Consequently, deploying $\pi$ only when
$n^{-1}\sum_iY_i-r_n(\delta)>0$ has false-improvement probability at most $\delta$. If Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp} leaves population ambiguity $[a,b]$ around the identified component, adding $[a,b]$ to the sampling interval remains valid.
\end{proposition}

This proposition applies to signed PDIS directly and, conditional on an independent nuisance-training split, to the augmented contribution introduced next. Thus efficiency improvements can translate into stronger certificates without weakening finite-sample validity.

\section{Efficient Contrast Estimation Without Separate Values}

Write $\omega_t=W_t^\Delta$ and set $v_{H+1}^\Delta=0$. Define the \emph{contrastive Bellman recursion} entirely under the logged law:
\begin{align}
q_t^\Delta(z_t)
&=\bbE_\beta[\omega_tR_t+\gamma v_{t+1}^\Delta(H_{t+1})\mid Z_t=z_t],
\label{eq:contrastq}\\
v_t^\Delta(h_t)
&=\bbE_{A_t\sim\beta_t(\cdot\mid h_t)}[q_t^\Delta(h_t,A_t)].
\label{eq:contrastv}
\end{align}
Repeated conditioning gives $\DeltaV=\bbE_\beta[v_1^\Delta(H_1)]$. Crucially, $q_t^\Delta$ and $v_t^\Delta$ are logged-law objects. They can be identified under \UCC even when the candidate and baseline value functions are not separately identified.

For arbitrary fitted functions $\widetilde q_t$, define their known-logger action averages
$\widetilde v_t(h_t)=\bbE_{A_t\sim\beta_t}[\widetilde q_t(h_t,A_t)]$
and $\widetilde v_{H+1}=0$. The \emph{contrastive augmented PDIS} contribution is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
Y_{\rm CA}(\widetilde q)
&=\widetilde v_1(H_1)+\sum_{t=1}^H\gamma^{t-1}\bigl\{\omega_tR_t\\
&\qquad+\gamma\widetilde v_{t+1}(H_{t+1})-\widetilde q_t(Z_t)\bigr\}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:capdis}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Contrastive augmentation and efficiency]
\label{thm:efficiency}
Assume \UCC and known $\beta$.
For every fixed $\widetilde q$, $\bbE_\beta Y_{\rm CA}(\widetilde q)=\DeltaV$; hence the same holds conditionally for a nuisance model fitted on independent data. At the oracle recursion $q^\Delta$, $Y_{\rm CA}(q^\Delta)-\DeltaV$ is the efficient influence function in the nonparametric sequential model with known logging mechanism. Writing $\mathcal V_{\rm eff}=\operatorname{Var}_\beta\{Y_{\rm CA}(q^\Delta)\}$ and
\[
e_t=(\widetilde q_t-q_t^\Delta)-(\widetilde v_t-v_t^\Delta),
\]
we have the exact identity
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Var}_\beta\{Y_{\rm CA}(\widetilde q)\}
=\mathcal V_{\rm eff}+
\sum_{t=1}^H\gamma^{2(t-1)}\bbE_\beta[e_t^2].
\label{eq:generalvariance}
\end{equation}
In particular, setting $\widetilde q=0$ yields
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Var}_\beta(Y_{\rm PDIS})
=\mathcal V_{\rm eff}+
\sum_{t=1}^H\gamma^{2(t-1)}
\bbE_\beta[(q_t^\Delta-v_t^\Delta)^2].
\label{eq:pdisvariance}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
With a fixed number of folds, the supplement further proves that cross-fitted CA-PDIS is asymptotically efficient whenever its action-centered continuation error converges to zero in $L_2$; unlike ordinary doubly robust remainders, no product-rate condition is needed.

The second term in Eq.~\eqref{eq:pdisvariance} is pure \emph{action-composition noise}: it arises because the known logger randomly chooses actions. The augmentation removes exactly this orthogonal component. With individually covered policies, Eq.~\eqref{eq:capdis} reduces to the difference of familiar sequential doubly robust estimators. Under contrastive coverage alone, the direct recursion remains defined while those two separate estimators need not be.
