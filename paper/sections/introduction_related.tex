\section{Introduction}

Offline reinforcement learning must make decisions from data collected by a fixed logging policy. Its central obstacle is coverage: a target policy may visit actions or trajectories absent from the log, leaving its value unidentified. Consequently, off-policy evaluation (OPE), pessimistic policy learning, and safe policy improvement usually require coverage of the target policy or constrain the learned policy near a covered baseline \citep{precup2000eligibility,thomas2015high,laroche2019safe,rashidinejad2021bridging,xie2021bellman}.

Deployment, however, rarely requires two absolute policy values. The operational question is often whether a candidate improves on an existing baseline,
\begin{equation}
\DeltaV := V^\pi-V^{\pi_0}>0.
\label{eq:contrast}
\end{equation}
The distinction matters. Suppose both policies take the same unsupported action with probability one half, but differ on two actions well covered by the logger. The reward of the unsupported action prevents identifying either value, yet it cancels exactly in their difference. Conventional separate-value analysis discards this cancellation and can remain vacuous even with infinite data.

Pairwise OPE has exploited covariance to estimate differences more precisely under overlap \citep{jeunen2024delta}, and offline policy-comparison benchmarks evaluate confidence in pairwise queries \citep{koul2022offline}. Our first question is more basic: \emph{what support is required to identify the comparison at all?} The answer cannot be expressed only through marginal state-action coverage. An unsupported excursion can leave and later re-enter an observed state, so cancellation must hold along complete policy paths.

We introduce \emph{uniform contrastive coverage} (\UCC). For every state-action history, if its likelihood under the logger is zero, its likelihood must be identical under the candidate and baseline. The condition permits arbitrary common off-support behavior but forbids unmatched off-support path mass. Identification is only the first step: naive signed importance sampling can retain large variance from the logger's randomized action composition. We therefore derive a direct contrastive Bellman recursion whose continuation functions are identifiable even when the two policies' ordinary value functions are not.

Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We prove that \UCC is necessary and sufficient for uniform nonparametric identification of $V^\pi-V^{\pi_0}$ over finite-horizon controlled processes with unrestricted bounded rewards. The proof yields a signed importance-weight representation and a two-step counterexample showing why local action agreement is insufficient.
    \item We establish strict separation from individual coverage, characterize policy-library equivalence classes, and identify an important boundary: when $\pi_0$ is the logger, \UCC collapses exactly to ordinary coverage of $\pi$.
    \item For deterministic history trees, we derive a sharp partial-identification interval. Its width equals the discounted $\ell_1$ mass of the unmatched target-minus-baseline path measure on logger-null histories, giving the strongest uniformly valid population abstention rule in this model.
    \item We introduce contrastive augmented PDIS (CA-PDIS). It is conditionally unbiased for any split-fitted continuation model, has the efficient influence function at the oracle continuation, and admits an exact excess-variance formula.
    \item We extend fixed-pair certification to a searched policy library: a structural signature audit screens unidentified comparisons, while simultaneous empirical-Bernstein lower bounds control family-wise false deployment. New CPU-only experiments show why both stages are necessary and quantify the power gained from augmentation.
\end{enumerate}

Our goal is not a new deep-RL benchmark score. It is a theorem-driven account of when offline data can answer a policy-improvement query that standard OPE cannot answer policy by policy, and how to answer that query efficiently and safely after policy search.

\section{Related Work}

\paragraph{OPE, minimax risk, and efficient inference.}
Classical importance sampling changes measure from the logging policy to a target policy under overlap \citep{precup2000eligibility}. Regression and importance-weighted estimators have been compared through minimax risk in bandits and finite-horizon MDPs \citep{li2015minimax}; fitted-Q methods admit instance-dependent and nearly matching minimax guarantees under linear approximation \citep{duan2020minimax}. Doubly robust estimators combine outcome models with propensity weighting \citep{dudik2011doubly,jiang2016doubly,thomas2016data}, while marginalized density-ratio and double-reinforcement-learning methods reduce horizon dependence and can attain semiparametric efficiency in Markov or linear models \citep{liu2018breaking,nachum2019dualdice,uehara2020minimax,kallus2020double,xie2023efficient}. Confidence intervals have been developed through high-confidence importance weighting, debiasing, and bootstrap FQE \citep{thomas2015high,shi2021deeply,hao2021bootstrap}. These methods principally target an absolute policy value and require that the relevant target distribution be dominated by the data distribution. Our efficient object is instead the continuation of a \emph{signed policy contrast}; the separate target continuations may not exist because common unsupported behavior cancels only after subtraction.

\paragraph{Pairwise evaluation, policy selection, and repeated search.}
Direct difference estimation can exploit the covariance between two policy estimates \citep{jeunen2024delta}, and policy-comparison benchmarks emphasize calibrated pairwise decisions \citep{koul2022offline}. Offline policy selection has also been studied through self-normalized lower bounds, confidence sequences, Bayesian value distributions, PAC-Bayes guarantees, and recent second-order bounds \citep{kuzborskij2021confident,karampatziakis2021confidence,yang2022selection,sakhi2023pac,ryu2025secondorder}. This literature addresses statistical uncertainty when selecting among candidates. We add an orthogonal support question: before correcting for multiplicity, which pairwise values are identified at all? Our signature classes answer this structurally, and Theorem~\ref{thm:library} then supplies a simple finite-sample family-wise certificate inside an identifiable class.

\paragraph{Safe policy improvement.}
Safe policy iteration maximizes lower bounds on improvement, and SPIBB reverts toward a data-collecting baseline in uncertain regions \citep{pirotta2013safe,laroche2019safe}. Pessimistic offline RL similarly avoids unsupported actions or models \citep{rashidinejad2021bridging,xie2021bellman,jin2022policy}. These methods are especially natural when the comparison baseline is the logger. Proposition~\ref{prop:logger} shows that no cancellation gain is available in that boundary case: contrastive coverage becomes ordinary target coverage. Our setting is complementary---a third-party logger compares two systems that may share an unsupported controller, fallback, or escalation path.

\paragraph{Beyond overlap and partial identification.}
Partial identification reports all values compatible with observed data and maintained assumptions \citep{manski2003partial}. Interval OPE characterizes irreducible uncertainty without sufficient exploration or realizability \citep{miyaguchi2023interval}; smoothness can bound unsupported actions \citep{khan2023beyond}; weak-overlap estimators relax bounded ratios but retain target-policy domination \citep{mehrabi2024weak}. Incremental interventions and positivity-free policy classes change the estimand or restrict the policy class to avoid unsupported interventions \citep{kennedy2019incremental,zhao2023positivity}. We keep the candidate and baseline fixed, impose no smooth extrapolation, and exploit a different source of identification: equality of their off-support signed path mass. On deterministic history trees, the resulting interval is sharp and its width has an exact unmatched-mass formula.

\paragraph{Known versus estimated logging mechanisms.}
Estimating the logging policy from the evaluation sample can paradoxically reduce importance-sampling variance by correcting random action-count fluctuations \citep{hanna2019importance,zhou2025demystifying}. Our decomposition isolates the same action-composition source but assumes the logger is known. CA-PDIS removes this orthogonal component explicitly through the known-logger action projection, preserving exact split-sample unbiasedness for any continuation model. This distinction is useful operationally: model misspecification affects power, not validity, while the held-out certificate remains finite-sample safe.

\paragraph{Summary of the distinction.}
Standard and efficient OPE estimate an absolute value under target domination; pairwise OPE improves precision under overlap; safe-improvement and selection methods control deployment risk; and partial-identification methods bound unsupported effects. Our contribution is different at the support level: the signed prefix difference itself may be dominated even when neither target distribution is, after which efficient and simultaneous inference can be built for that contrast.
