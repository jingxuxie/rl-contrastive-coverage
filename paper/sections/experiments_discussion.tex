\section{Experiments}

We test only theorem-predicted phenomena. All experiments are CPU-only, use exact tabular calculations or i.i.d. Monte Carlo sampling, and are reproducible from seed 20260726. No online return is used for tuning; the supplement specifies every process, grid, range calculation, and repetition count.

\paragraph{Identification and sharpness.}
In the three-action bandit, $\beta=(.5,.5,0)$ and supported reward means are $(.8,.2)$. Candidate and baseline share unsupported mass $q$, perturbed by mismatch $\epsilon$. Figure~\ref{fig:population}a confirms that sharp contrastive width is exactly $|\epsilon|$, independent of $q$: at $q=.4,\epsilon=0$, its width is zero while separate-value bounds have width $.8$. Across 5,000 random ten-action bandits, the width identity has maximum error $1.9\times10^{-16}$; the median and 90th-percentile separate/contrastive ratios are $7.3$ and $36.4$ (Figure~\ref{fig:stress}a). In a five-step chain with a shared logger-null branch, width is exactly $|\epsilon|H$ to $6.7\times10^{-16}$. At zero mismatch the true improvement is $.6$, whereas separate population bounds $[-1.4,2.6]$ remain inconclusive even with infinite data. Random history trees independently verify Eq.~\eqref{eq:width} to $2.2\times10^{-16}$.

\paragraph{Efficiency and learned augmentation.}
We enumerate all logger action strings in binary chains through horizon 16 and integrate reward noise analytically. Equation~\eqref{eq:pdisvariance} holds to $2.1\times10^{-14}$; the PDIS/efficiency variance ratio grows from $2.19$ to $13.69$, so action composition accounts for $92.7\%$ of PDIS variance at $H=16$ (Figure~\ref{fig:stress}b). In an eight-step chain, two-fold CA-PDIS fits only two action-reward means. At $n=2{,}000$ it lowers MSE by $8.49\times$ with bias $1.22\times10^{-4}$. In a five-state, six-step stochastic MDP with a shared $.25$-probability logger-null branch, tabular CA-PDIS lowers MSE by $7.59\times$ at $n=5{,}000$ with bias $-2.12\times10^{-3}$; neither absolute policy value is identified.

\paragraph{Finite-sample deployment and search.}
For sequential certification, half the data fit the nuisance model and the untouched half evaluate both methods; contribution ranges are computed exactly by enumeration. At 1,000 total episodes, CA-PDIS certifies all 500 positive-improvement trials while signed PDIS certifies one, and both cover in every trial (Figure~\ref{fig:certificate}). We then search a fixed library of up to 100 policies that all share $.35$ mass on one logger-null action. Under a global null, maximizing uncorrected pointwise Wald bounds falsely deploys with probability $.927$ at $K=100$; Bonferroni Wald has empirical error $.027$, and both simultaneous empirical-Bernstein rules make no false deployment in 300 trials. With one improving candidate among 50, CA-PDIS--EB achieves safe-certification probability $.897$ at 6,000 episodes versus $.143$ for signed-PDIS--EB (Table~\ref{tab:librarymain}). This isolates three distinct requirements: contrastive support screening, multiplicity control, and efficient estimation.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}

Contrastive coverage is most useful when a third-party logger compares systems that share an unsupported fallback, escalation path, or controller. It does not create coverage for improving directly over the logger (Proposition~\ref{prop:logger}) and never extrapolates unmatched off-support effects. The uniform theorem deliberately permits arbitrary history-dependent environments; stronger structural assumptions may identify more comparisons, while explicit path auditing can be expensive in large spaces. The statistical results assume known propensities, independent bounded-reward episodes, and a fixed pair or finite library defined before the certification split. The efficient recursion permits general history-dependent nuisance models, but our validations use tabular classes. Estimated propensities, adaptively generating candidates after viewing certification data, continuous-action approximations, and fully cross-fitted finite-sample certificates remain open. Finally, the sharp sequential interval is proved for deterministic history trees; unknown off-support transitions can couple continuation ambiguities and require a more general robust dynamic program.

\section{Conclusion}

Coverage should be analyzed for the quantity being asked. For a policy-improvement query, the exact object is the signed candidate-minus-baseline path measure: common unsupported behavior can cancel even when neither value is identifiable. Uniform contrastive coverage characterizes that cancellation, sharp bounds quantify any remainder, and CA-PDIS plus simultaneous lower bounds turns identification into efficient, search-safe deployment.
