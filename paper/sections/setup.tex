\section{Setup and Contrastive Coverage}

We consider a finite-horizon controlled process of length $H$. At time $t$, the observed history is
$H_t=(S_1,A_1,\ldots,A_{t-1},S_t)$, a policy $\eta$ draws $A_t\sim\eta_t(\cdot\mid H_t)$, and a bounded reward $R_t\in[0,1]$ is observed. The discounted value is
$V_M^\eta=\bbE_M^\eta[\sum_{t=1}^H\gamma^{t-1}R_t]$ for environment $M$ and $\gamma\in(0,1]$. We observe $n$ independent episodes generated by a known logging policy $\beta$. The candidate $\pi$ and comparison baseline $\pi_0$ are fixed independently of the certification sample.

For an action-augmented history
$z_t=(s_1,a_1,\ldots,s_t,a_t)$, define the \emph{policy-path likelihood}
\begin{equation}
L_t^\eta(z_t)=\prod_{j=1}^t\eta_j(a_j\mid h_j).
\label{eq:pathlik}
\end{equation}
This contains only policy probabilities. Initial-state and transition factors are shared by all policies and are deliberately excluded.

\begin{definition}[Uniform contrastive coverage]
The pair $(\pi,\pi_0)$ satisfies \emph{uniform contrastive coverage} with respect to $\beta$ when, for every $t\le H$ and every formal history $z_t$,
\begin{equation}
L_t^\beta(z_t)=0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
L_t^\pi(z_t)=L_t^{\pi_0}(z_t).
\label{eq:ucc}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Thus the logger need not cover either policy separately. It must cover only their \emph{signed path difference}. The cumulative path requirement is essential: equality of local action probabilities after entering an unsupported region does not cancel different probabilities of reaching that region.

\begin{example}[Strictly weaker than individual coverage]
In a one-state bandit, let
$\beta=(.5,.5,0)$,
$\pi=(.4,.1,.5)$, and
$\pi_0=(.1,.4,.5)$.
The reward of action three is unobserved, so neither policy value is identified. Nevertheless,
$V^\pi-V^{\pi_0}=.3\{r(1)-r(2)\}$ is identified because the common unsupported mass cancels.
\end{example}

\begin{example}[Local agreement is insufficient]
At a two-step root, let the logger never take action one. The candidate and baseline take it with probabilities $.6$ and $.4$, then use identical actions inside the resulting unsupported state. An unknown terminal reward $r$ still contributes $.2r$ to the contrast. The local policies agree after entry, but their cumulative likelihoods of the unsupported path do not. This is why Eq.~\eqref{eq:ucc} is stated on prefixes rather than isolated state-action pairs.
\end{example}

\begin{proposition}[Logger-baseline boundary]
If $\pi_0=\beta$, then \UCC holds if and only if $L_t^\pi(z_t)>0$ implies $L_t^\beta(z_t)>0$ for every $t,z_t$.
\label{prop:logger}
\end{proposition}
This clarifies the scope of the gain. Contrastive coverage helps when two policies are compared against a third logger and share off-support behavior; it does not manufacture coverage for improving directly over the logger.

\paragraph{Checking and constructing the condition.}
For a finite history tree, \UCC can be checked by a depth-first traversal that propagates the three path likelihoods and flags any logger-null node with nonzero target-baseline difference. The naive cost is linear in the explicit tree size, which can still be exponential in $H$. In larger systems, Corollary~\ref{cor:mixture} gives a compositional sufficient condition: expose a shared controller or fallback module and verify coverage only for the distinct components. This is preferable to checking marginal state-action occupancy. Two policies may have equal marginal mass at a state but reach it through different unsupported histories, whose earlier rewards remain unidentified.

For a policy $\eta$, define its \emph{off-support signature} as the vector
$\Sigma_\beta(\eta)=\{L_t^\eta(z_t):L_t^\beta(z_t)=0,\ t\le H\}$,
using any fixed ordering of formal prefixes.

\begin{proposition}[Contrastive coverage classes]
\label{prop:classes}
The relation $\eta\equiv_\beta\xi$ defined by
$\Sigma_\beta(\eta)=\Sigma_\beta(\xi)$ is an equivalence relation, and a pair satisfies \UCC exactly when it lies in the same equivalence class. Consequently, a library of policies partitions into maximal classes whose every pairwise value difference is uniformly identified. All individually covered policies belong to the logger's zero-signature class, while policies can form other classes by sharing the same unsupported behavior.
\end{proposition}

On an explicit tree with $N$ prefix nodes, the signatures of $K$ policies can be propagated jointly in $O(KN)$ time and materialized in $O(KN)$ storage, rather than testing all pairs in $O(K^2N)$ time. The resulting audit is immediate: compare freely within a class; across classes, use the sharp unmatched-mass bounds or abstain.
